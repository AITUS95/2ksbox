## 1.2.0

- Windows 11 can use the **2ksbox adapter (experimental)**: Direct3D
  9, 10 and 11 games on your graphics card, and screen sizes up to
  3840x2160. It needs Windows in test mode, and the launcher asks
  first. On a Mac it works for Windows 11 on Arm too.
- On the 2ksbox adapter, Windows 11's screen follows the window's size.
- 3D frames stay on your graphics card on their way to the window, so
  games and the Windows 11 desktop run smoother.
- The player's **Machine** > **Disc** menu swaps the machine's disc, and
  **View** > **Shader** changes its CRT look while it runs. Every
  shader picker has **Off**.
- Windows 11's own mouse pointers look sharp at every display scaling.
- Games that use shadow maps draw their shadows on Windows XP, 98 and
  11. Gothic has its distance fog.
- Grand Theft Auto: Vice City no longer freezes on Windows 98.
- On Windows, renaming a disc on the shelf works, Escape cancels the
  rename, and cloning a machine shows its progress.
- Starting a machine that is already running says so.
- Many fixes from a security and reliability review.

## 1.1.0

- Windows 11 on Arm starts from the Mac App Store app. It stopped at
  "Guest has not initialized the display yet", because macOS keeps the
  app's firmware files compressed.
- 2ksbox asks where to keep your machines the first time it starts,
  and *Help* > **Choose Machines Folder…** changes it later. In the Mac
  App Store app this also makes **Browse…** open in a machine's own
  folder.
- The Mac's folder dialog has a **New Folder** button.
- A launcher with no machines yet opens at its full width.
- In the Mac App Store app, a machine on the network no longer lags,
  and its name lookups work. Windows 11 was slowest while it downloaded
  and installed updates.

## 1.0.0

The first release, on the Mac App Store and the Microsoft Store.

### Machines

- A launcher that makes Windows 98, Windows XP, Windows 7, Windows 11,
  DOS and other machines, each with hardware that suits it. Each
  machine opens in its own window.
- Windows 98 and DOS are emulated, on purpose, so old games see the
  kind of PC they were written for. On Windows and Linux, Windows XP
  and 7 can run on your computer's hypervisor.
- Windows 11 installs from Microsoft's own ISO with Secure Boot and a
  TPM 2.0, and shares the clipboard with your computer. On a Mac or a
  Linux computer it can share a folder too. On a Mac it is Windows 11
  on Arm.
- Snapshots of a machine turned off or running, kept as a tree, and
  clones.

### Games

- Direct3D from DirectX 1 to 9 on your graphics card, through the
  2ksbox adapter's driver on Windows 98 and XP.
- OpenGL games on your graphics card, through one file next to the
  game.
- An emulated 3dfx Voodoo 2 for Glide games, with 3dfx's own driver,
  from Windows 98 and from DOS.
- Disc images in cue/bin, CloneCD, Alcohol and ISO, or a folder, with
  copy-protected discs and CD audio. The disc shelf swaps discs while
  a machine runs.
- Sound Blaster 16 and General MIDI music, and the Roland MT-32 with
  ROMs you supply.
- USB gamepads, a gameport joystick, and a gamepad mapped to keyboard
  keys.

### The picture

- The guest's picture at its own resolution, scaled in whole steps,
  through a CRT shader you can tune and save as a profile per machine.

### Known limits

- The Mac App Store version needs macOS 26 and a Mac with Apple
  Silicon.
- Windows 7 is for its Aero desktop, not for games.
- Windows 11's display has no 3D acceleration. Its screen follows the
  window's size.
